"Papa, will we have to put the house up?"

If that question has come up at your dinner table, you're in good company, and it's a fair one to ask. An education loan for a Bachelor's, Master's, MBA or MS abroad is a serious commitment. Nobody should pledge the family home without understanding exactly what that means.

So here's an honest way to think about collateral and collateral-free education loans, before you sit down with any lender.

## What collateral actually means

Collateral is an asset you pledge to the lender as security for the loan. Depending on the lender, that could be a house, a flat or a plot of land, a fixed deposit, or another asset the lender is willing to accept.

While the loan is running, the lender has a legal claim on the asset, and for property it often keeps the original documents. Once the loan is fully repaid, the lender should release that claim and return your documents. If the loan isn't repaid as agreed, the lender may be able to use that claim to recover what's owed, which can include selling the asset, as set out in the loan agreement. That's why a secured loan deserves a proper family conversation, not a rushed yes.

When a loan is secured, the lender will usually want to check the asset first. For property, that can mean title documents, a valuation and a legal check, all done through the lender's own process.

> Before anyone signs, read the part of the loan agreement that covers the security.

## What collateral-free means

A collateral-free, or unsecured, education loan doesn't ask you to pledge an asset. Instead, the lender looks more closely at everything else. Lenders can look at your academic background, university, course, future employment prospects and your co-borrower's financial standing.

Some lenders may offer collateral-free options to students who meet their eligibility criteria. Each lender sets its own criteria, and they can differ a lot from one lender to the next.

**No collateral does not mean guaranteed approval.** It means the lender is weighing your profile rather than a pledged asset, and the decision still sits with the lender.

One more thing families often miss: collateral-free does not always mean co-borrower-free. Many lenders still ask for a co-borrower, usually a parent, and each lender sets its own rules on who that can be.

## What can differ between the two

It's natural to ask which one is better. The honest answer is that neither is right for everyone. What changes is the shape of the offer, and that depends on the lender and on your profile.

Here's what can differ:

- **How much a lender is willing to offer.** Collateral can shape the amount and terms a lender is willing to consider.
- **Interest, fees and other charges.** Each lender sets its own. Compare the full cost in writing, not one headline number.
- **Your own contribution.** Some lenders expect the family to pay part of the cost themselves, often called margin money.
- **The paperwork.** A secured loan adds asset documents and checks to the process.
- **The risk your family carries.** With collateral, a specific asset is on the line. Without it, no asset is pledged, but the co-borrower is usually just as responsible as the student for repaying the whole loan, and missed payments can affect your credit histories.

## Questions to talk through as a family

Before you approach any lender, sit down together and answer these honestly, student and parent both.

1. **Do we have an asset we're comfortable pledging?** Not just whether you own one, but whether everyone at the table is at peace with it being security for a loan.
2. **How much do we actually need?** Work out your full cost and funding gap first. Our guide on [how to estimate your study abroad cost and funding gap](/blog/study-abroad-cost-and-funding-gap) walks you through it.
3. **Who will co-borrow?** Lenders look at the co-borrower's financial standing, so that person needs to be part of the conversation from the start.
4. **What will repayment look like?** Ask each lender when repayment starts, whether interest builds up while you study, and what happens if you want to repay early.
5. **What's our plan B?** If one route doesn't work out, it helps to know your other options before a fee deadline gets close.

## Three myths worth clearing up

- **"Collateral-free means anyone can get it."** It doesn't. No collateral does not mean guaranteed approval.
- **"If we pledge property, approval is certain."** It isn't. A secured loan is still the lender's decision.
- **"One type of loan is always the right choice."** That depends on your course, your university, your co-borrower, your family's comfort with risk and what each lender offers.
