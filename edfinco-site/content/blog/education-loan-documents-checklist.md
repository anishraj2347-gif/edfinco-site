Paperwork is the least exciting part of funding a degree abroad. It's also the part that can quietly hold everything up. A missing page, an old address or a name spelled two different ways can mean extra questions and extra waiting.

The good news: most of it is predictable. Here's a general checklist of what students and co-borrowers are often asked for when applying for an abroad education loan.

Before you start, one important note: **every lender has its own list.** Requirements vary by lender, by type of loan and by your profile. Treat this as a starting point and always go by the lender's own checklist.

## Three habits that make this easier

- **Keep one folder.** A digital folder and a paper file, with every document named clearly, such as "Co-borrower PAN" rather than "scan001".
- **Check that details match.** Names, dates of birth and addresses should match across your passport, IDs and academic records. If something doesn't, ask the lender how to explain it.
- **Scan cleanly.** Clear, complete scans of every page. A cropped or blurry scan just means sending it again.

## Student documents

### Identity and address

- Valid passport
- PAN card, and Aadhaar or another ID the lender accepts
- Proof of current address, if it differs from your ID
- Recent passport-size photographs

### Academic records

- Class 10 and Class 12 mark sheets and pass certificates
- For a Master's, MBA or MS, your degree certificate (or your results so far, if you're still finishing) plus mark sheets or transcripts for each semester or year
- Scores from any tests your university required, such as GRE, GMAT, SAT, IELTS, TOEFL or PTE
- Your CV and work experience letters, if you've worked
- A short note explaining any gap in your studies, if the lender asks for one

## Admission documents

- **Your admission or offer letter** from the university. If the offer is conditional, ask the lender how it handles that.
- **The university's cost of attendance or fee details,** showing tuition and, where given, estimated living costs. Lenders may use these to understand how much you need.
- **Scholarship, assistantship or funding letters,** if you have any. Confirmed funding can reduce how much you need to borrow.
- **Country-specific documents,** such as an I-20 for the USA or a CAS for the UK. Your university may need proof of funds, which can include a loan sanction letter, before it issues these, and some lenders only ask for them later, before disbursement. Check the order with both.

## Co-borrower documents

Many lenders ask for a co-borrower, usually a parent, and each lender sets its own rules on who can co-borrow. Whoever it is, whether that's Papa, Mummy or another family member the lender accepts, their paperwork matters as much as yours. Bring them in early.

### Identity and relationship

- PAN card, and Aadhaar or another ID the lender accepts
- Proof of current address
- Recent passport-size photographs
- Proof of relationship to the student, if the lender asks for it

### Existing loans and credit

- Details of any existing loans, such as a home loan or car loan, with recent statements

Lenders may also check the co-borrower's credit history, so it can help to look at your own credit report before you apply.

## Financial documents

Ask the lender how many months or years of each document it needs.

### If the co-borrower is salaried

- Recent salary slips
- Form 16 (your employer's TDS certificate) and income tax returns (ITRs) for recent years
- Bank statements for recent months, showing salary credits
- An employment letter, if the lender asks for one

### If the co-borrower is self-employed or runs a business

- Income tax returns (ITRs) with computation of income for recent years
- Audited financial statements, such as the balance sheet and profit and loss account, where applicable
- Proof that the business exists, such as a GST registration or other business registration
- Business and personal bank statements for recent months

### Collateral documents, only if you're offering security

If your loan will be secured, the lender may ask for:

- Property documents, such as the title or sale deed and earlier ownership documents
- An approved building plan, where applicable, and recent property tax receipts
- An encumbrance certificate from the sub-registrar's office, which lists transactions registered against the property over a set period, such as a sale or a registered mortgage
- Fixed deposit details, if a deposit is being pledged instead

The lender may also arrange its own valuation and legal check of the property.

Not every loan needs this section. Some lenders may offer collateral-free options to students who meet their eligibility criteria, but **no collateral does not mean guaranteed approval,** and the lender decides whether security is needed. Our guide to [collateral vs collateral-free education loans](/blog/collateral-vs-collateral-free-education-loans) explains the difference.

## A few final tips

- **Don't hand over originals unless the lender asks.** Share copies for the application. The lender may ask to see originals to verify them, and for a secured loan it may keep the original property documents until the loan is repaid, so ask for a written list of every original you hand over.
- **Share documents only with people you trust.** Ask who will see your papers and how they'll be used.
- **Keep a simple log** of what you sent, to whom and when.
- **Ask before you guess.** If you're not sure whether a document applies to you, ask. A question now saves a resubmission later.
